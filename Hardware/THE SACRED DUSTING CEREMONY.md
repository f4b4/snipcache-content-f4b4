# THE SACRED DUSTING CEREMONY

Every morning, before a single line of code is written, before the IDE is opened, the ritual begins.

1. THE SCHNUPFERBESEN — A hand-crafted Bavarian snuff brush, acquired at the Victualienmarkt in Munich, now a twenty-year-old heirloom. Horsehair bristles, two-toned (black and grayish-brown), anti-static by design. It is NOT a desk broom. It is a SCHNUPFERBESEN. It flicks dust decisively where synthetic bristles only smear.

2. THE KEYBOARD & MOUSEPAD — Dusted first, every single day. On Mondays or after holidays, the ceremony expands: monitors, monitor tops, monitor backs. All dust must be banished.

3. THE DISTILLED WATER — Purchased in five-liter canisters, transferred into small Japanese spray bottles of various sizes. Not monitor cleaning solution. Not glass cleaner. Just distilled water — because lime is the enemy of screens, and every commercial cleaner leaves a film.

4. THE TEMPO POCKET TISSUE — The final, crucial instrument. Superior to kitchen paper. Superior to microfiber. Tempo tissues, because they don't leave lint and they don't scratch.

5. THE STABILO BOSS — Kept always at hand. When someone — usually a manager — insists on touching the monitor while discussing source code, the Stabilo Boss is produced. The cap end is pointed at the screen. Fingers are forbidden.
